how it works

A loop that never sleeps

No score, no prediction, no setting a person can tune per stream. The rule fits in a terminal window and every number on this page comes out of it.

eight steps, swipe

01
  .--------.
  | > chat |  anon
  '--------'  read-only

Join

The agent joins the stream's chat as an anonymous reader. It can see messages. It cannot send one.

kick · twitch · no account
02
  |##|##|##|##|##|##|
  10s 10s 10s 10s 10s

Count

Messages are counted in ten-second ticks aligned to the clock, so anyone replaying the chat gets the same counts.

epoch-aligned · deterministic
03
  rate     = tick / 10
  baseline = last 60s / 60

Rate

The stream is rated only against itself: the last complete tick over its own minute before. Not against a number we picked, not against another stream.

self-relative · no global scale
04
  ----------------- 3x
          ^ you are here

The line

Three times the minute before, and at least two messages a second. Under that a quiet chat with one joke does not fire.

3.0x · floor 2/s
05
  [ring 120s] -> [30s] -> mp4
  ffmpeg, no re-encode

Cut

Over the line, the newest thirty seconds leave the ring buffer as a clip, with a thumbnail and the numbers that caused it.

streamlink · ffmpeg · 480p
06
  clips.json
  posted: false

Queue

The clip lands in a public queue on the coin's page. Someone with the coin's account posts it, or does not. The agent has no account.

human posts · agent never
07
  cooldown ######## 90s

Cooldown

One cut per crossing. After a spike the stream cannot fire again for ninety seconds, so one moment is one clip.

90 s · one moment, one clip
08
  every hour:
  collect -> 40 / 40 / 20

Fees

Once an hour the platform wallet collects every coin's creator fees and splits them. Every transfer gets a signature in the log.

burn · launchers · platform
# agent/detector.py, the whole rule. open source, one function, tested.
rate       = messages in the last complete tick / 10
baseline   = messages in the 6 ticks before it / 60
multiplier = rate / max(baseline, 0.3)
SPIKE      = multiplier >= 3.0 and rate >= 2.0 and not cooling
WARM       = multiplier >= 1.5
cooldown   = 90 s after a SPIKE
warm-up    = 70 s after joining: no verdict before the minute is seen

economics

Where the fees go

Every coin on the wall is created by the platform wallet, so every coin's creator fees land in one place. Once an hour they are split three ways and every transaction is written to a public log.

Open the fee log →
40%

buys and burns $WALL

Swapped on Jupiter, burned in the same round, both signatures in the log. The wall's volume becomes $WALL supply leaving forever.

40%

to the launchers

Whoever launched a coin gets a share every hour, pro rata by that coin's trading volume in the hour. Launch a stream that moves and you are paid for it.

20%

runs the agent

Server, ring buffers, encoding. Sent to the platform's cold wallet, which is never on a server. Also in the log.

the platform coin

$WALL

One coin for the whole wall. It is not tied to a stream. It is tied to all of them.

what it does

  • burned every hour. 40% of every coin's creator fees buy $WALL and burn it. More streams on the wall, more fees, more burn.
  • the key to the launch page. Launching a coin requires holding $WALL. The amount: set on launch day. No hold, no launch.
  • nothing else. No staking page, no points, no promises. The burn log is the whole story and it is public.

what it is not

  • Not a share of anything. Holding $WALL does not pay you. The burn is the only mechanism.
  • Not controlled by the agent. The hot wallet buys and burns on a timer with public signatures. It cannot sell $WALL. The code has no sell path.
  • Not launched twice. One mint, posted from the project account on launch day. Any other address with this name is not ours.

hard limits

What it will never do

These are not settings. They are things the code cannot do, and you can read the code.

Agent source →

never posts

The agent cuts a clip and puts it in a queue. It has no X account, no Kick account, no Twitch account. A person decides what goes out and from where.

never trades

The hot wallet creates coins, makes the first buy, collects fees and burns $WALL. It does not buy, sell or hold anyone's coin after the first launch buy.

never predicts

Counts, rates and ratios. No score, no target, no "about to pump". A verdict is a number compared with another number.

never tunes per stream

One rule, one set of numbers, printed above. There is no per-stream setting in the config and no way to add one without a commit.

never hides a transaction

Every fee movement is in fees.json with a signature you can open on Solscan. Every clip has the counts that caused it.

never holds the cold key

The hot wallet holds pocket money and runs on the box. The platform's share goes to a cold wallet that no server has ever seen.

questions

Straight answers

Why tie a coin to a stream?

A live stream is live attention. The chat is a number anyone can watch. The coin gets a reason to move that is not a tweet from the deployer: the room itself. When the room goes off, the clip exists within seconds and the coin's page shows it.

Who posts the clips?

A person with the coin's account. The agent only cuts and queues. If nobody posts, the clip stays on the coin page with its numbers. We are not building a bot that spams X.

What does a launcher get?

40% of the fees, split every hour between launchers by their coin's trading volume in that hour. The transfer has a signature in the fee log. Your wallet is the launcher of record from the moment the coin is created.

Does the streamer have to agree?

No. The agent reads a public chat anonymously and clips a public stream. The streamer can see the coin page like anyone else. If a streamer asks us to take their stream off the wall, we take it off. That is a rule, not a setting.

What happens when the stream goes offline?

The card says offline, the rate drops to zero, nothing cuts. The coin keeps trading on pump.fun like any other coin. When the stream comes back, the agent rejoins and the warm-up starts again.

Can the agent be wrong?

It can cut a boring moment if the chat spams. It cannot cut a moment the chat ignored. Every cut carries its counts, so a wrong cut is visible as a wrong cut. We would rather be wrong in public than right in private.

Is the agent open source?

Yes. The rule is one function with tests. The clipper is streamlink and ffmpeg. The fee round is one script on a timer. You can run the whole thing on a $5 box and get the same numbers.
